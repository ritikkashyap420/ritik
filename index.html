Thesis & Approach

I believe the next trillion-dollar companies won't be building SaaS tools for other SaaS tools. They will be moving atoms, solving hard engineering problems, or rebuilding computing infrastructure from the ground up.

When assessing early-stage technical risk, I look for extreme clarity of thought and an obsessive focus on fundamental physics or mathematical truths, rather than market trends.

  • What is the hard technical problem? Don't abstract it. Explain the physics/math.
  • Why now? What specific breakthrough enables this today?
  • Why you? Your unique insight or obsession with this problem.
  • Link to a demo, paper, or repo. Avoid long pitch decks.

Mistakes & Learnings

Over-indexing on pedigree over obsession.

Early on, I backed a team solely because of their FAANG background. They built a great product for a non-existent problem. Learning: Look for founders who are irrationally obsessed with the problem space, not just technically capable.

Underestimating distribution in deeptech.

Assuming a 10x better hardware product sells itself is a fallacy. Learning: Even in deeptech, founders must have a clear thesis on go-to-market and early pilot partnerships from day zero.
